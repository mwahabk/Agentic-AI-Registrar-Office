## Recommended course sequence

First year: CS101, MATH150, MATH160 and ENG101. Second year: CS102, then CS201 and CS220 once CS102 is passed, plus electives such as CS150 or MATH260. Third year: CS210 and electives in a chosen area. Final year: CS400 Senior Capstone and remaining electives. Take core courses as early as their prerequisites allow, because CS400 needs both CS201 and CS220.

## Choosing electives by interest

- Artificial intelligence and machine learning: CS310 Artificial Intelligence, then CS320 Machine Learning (also needs MATH250). MATH260 helps.
- Data science: CS260 Data Mining, CS220 Databases, MATH260 Probability and Statistics.
- Systems: CS210 Computer Systems, then CS301 Operating Systems and CS330 Computer Networks.
- Cybersecurity: CS240 Computer Security, plus CS210 and CS330.
- Software development: CS150 Web Programming and CS250 Software Engineering.
- Computing for biology and health: CS340 Bioinformatics.

## Students on probation or at risk

A student whose cumulative GPA is below 2.0 is on academic probation and limited to 13 credits. Advisors should help the student repeat failed courses first, because the new grade replaces the old one in the GPA, choose a lighter load of courses the student is well prepared for, and meet at least twice during the semester. A student on probation cannot take an overload.

## Planning to graduate

Check degree progress each semester: 42 credits, a GPA of at least 2.0, and all eight core courses. Courses in progress count toward the plan but not toward earned credits until graded. If a needed course is full, register for another section next semester or ask to be notified when a seat opens; the online assistant cannot add students to a full section.

## When to contact the Registrar office in person

The online assistant cannot approve overloads, waive prerequisites, change posted grades, process withdrawals after the deadline or handle questions the handbook does not cover. For those, students should contact the Registrar office or their academic dean.

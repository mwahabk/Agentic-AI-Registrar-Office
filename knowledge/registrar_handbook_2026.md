## Registration periods

Registration for Spring 2027 opens on October 26, 2026 and stays open until the last day to add a class. Students register through the Registrar office or the online Registrar assistant. Only the upcoming semester (Spring 2027) is open for registration; past semesters and the current semester (Fall 2026) cannot be changed by registering.

## Spring 2027 calendar and deadlines

- Registration opens: October 26, 2026.
- Classes begin: January 19, 2027.
- Last day to add a class: January 26, 2027.
- Last day to drop a class with no record on the transcript: February 2, 2027.
- Last day to withdraw from a class with a W grade: March 26, 2027.
- Final exams: May 10-14, 2027. Final grades are due May 18, 2027.

Fall 2026 final grades are due December 15, 2026. Only the professor teaching a section can submit its grades, and only for the current semester.

## Credit load and overloads

A full-time undergraduate load is 12 to 18 credits per semester. Students may register for at most 18 credits. Registering for more than 18 credits is an overload and requires written permission from the student's academic dean; overload requests are not processed by the online assistant. Students on academic warning or academic probation can never take an overload.

## Academic standing and probation

Academic standing is based on the cumulative GPA.

- Good standing: cumulative GPA of 2.0 or higher.
- Probation watch: cumulative GPA from 2.0 up to 2.3. No restrictions, but the student should meet their advisor.
- Academic probation: cumulative GPA below 2.0. The student is at risk of dismissal and may register for at most 13 credits per semester. Students on probation should repeat failed courses first and meet their advisor before registering.
- Academic suspension: a student who stays below a 2.0 cumulative GPA after a semester on probation is suspended for at least one semester and must apply for re-admission.
- Honors: cumulative GPA of 3.5 or higher.

## Prerequisites

A prerequisite counts only after it is completed with a grade of C- or better. Courses still in progress do not count: a student taking CS210 this semester cannot register for CS301 until the CS210 grade is posted. A grade of D+, D or F in a prerequisite does not satisfy it. Students who believe a prerequisite should be waived may petition the department head; the online assistant cannot grant prerequisite overrides.

## Repeating a course

A course may be repeated only if the earlier grade was D+, D or F. A course completed with C- or better cannot be taken again for credit. When a course is repeated, it counts once toward credits and GPA, using the most recent grade; the earlier attempt stays on the transcript marked as repeated. A student cannot register for a course they are currently taking.

## Grading scale and GPA

Grade points per credit: A 4.0, A- 3.7, B+ 3.3, B 3.0, B- 2.7, C+ 2.3, C 2.0, C- 1.7, D+ 1.3, D 1.0, F 0.0. The GPA is the total grade points divided by the total graded credits. Courses in progress (IP) do not count until a grade is posted. An F earns no credit; D+ and D earn credit but do not satisfy prerequisites.

## Time conflicts and seat limits

A student cannot register for two classes that meet at the same time. Classes conflict when they share a meeting day and their times overlap, even partly (for example MWF 09:00-09:50 and MW 09:30-10:45). Each section has a seat limit (35 seats); when a section is full, registration is refused. Registration checks seats and saves the enrollment as one locked step, so two students can never take the same last seat.

## Graduation requirements

To graduate with the B.S. in Computer Science (sample program), a student needs 42 credits, a cumulative GPA of at least 2.0, and all eight core courses: CS101, CS102, CS201, CS220, CS400, MATH150, MATH160 and ENG101. Every passed course, core or elective, counts toward the 42 credits. Students should apply for graduation by the end of the second week of their final semester.

## Records privacy (FERPA)

Student records are private. Students can see only their own transcript, schedule and degree progress. Advisors can see the records of their own advisees. Professors can see class lists and grade statistics only for sections they teach. Registrar administrators can see all records and university-wide statistics. Records are never shared with other students.
